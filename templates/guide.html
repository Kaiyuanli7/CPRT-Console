Guide

The method behind the numbers, the caveats to mention in Q&A, and the ground rules.

About this guide

This page is for explaining the data to judges. For using the console day to day, what each page shows and what to do when something goes wrong, see How to use.

How the Copart scan works

Copart's search pages show counts for the whole site next to the lots they list: how many lots each yard has and how many were added in the last 24 hours and 7 days. The scan opens two public pages in a normal, logged-out browser: all lots, and lots added in the last 7 days. It saves those counts and the raw responses. Adding up the yards gives each state's listed inventory and new-lot flow, and comparing scans a week apart gives the trend.

Say this in Q&A: the counts cover every kind of seller, because Copart hides sellers from logged-out visitors. "Added in the last 7 days" is Copart's own filter. It counts lots assigned to a sale in the last 7 days that are still listed, so lots that sold within the week are missing. Treat it as a consistent weekly proxy for new assignments, not a count of every arrival.

If Copart shows the automated browser a bot check, the scan stops for the day and tells you. It never tries to get around the check. To fill a gap, use Scan with me on Copart inventory: you open one filter in a Copart window and the console saves the same counts.

How the insurer tracker collects data

Recorded sessions. "Record on Copart" (or IAA) opens a browser window. Browse like a normal visitor: search one state, open a few pages of results, move to the next state, then close the window. The console keeps the listing data your browser received, works out which field holds the lot number, seller, state and vehicle, and saves every lot. Each results page with enough lots is added to your saved pages.

Automatic capture. The weekly update revisits your saved pages at a slow, human pace. If a site shows a bot check, the console stops for that site until next week rather than trying to get around it. Record a new session whenever you want to add states or refresh pages.

By hand. For anything the recorder can't reach, paste a listing's text on Collect data. The form fills itself; check it and save.

What the split means

Paused for now. In September 2026 Copart showed the seller on about 1 lot in 20 to logged-out visitors, IAA's pages carried no lot data the console can read, and IAA's robots.txt asks automated tools to stay out of its search. The split can't be measured from public pages, so it stays hidden until a site shows sellers. The Copart scan measures volumes instead.

For each insurer, the split is the share of its listed salvage lots that are on Copart rather than IAA. It only counts states sampled on both sites that week, because a state sampled on one site only would skew it. The white whisker is a 95% confidence interval: with 40 lots, a 55% share could plausibly be anywhere from about 40% to 69%. A week-over-week change is marked significant only when it's unlikely to be sampling noise.

Say this in Q&A: the split measures listed inventory, not contract terms or assignment counts. It's directional evidence that tests the expert-call claims (Progressive about 95% IAA, GEICO about 95% Copart) and shows whether share loss is spreading beyond Progressive. State Farm, the largest insurer, is the one to watch.

Signals

  • Total-loss pressure: repair prices divided by used-car prices, 2019 = 100. Rising means more wrecks get totaled.
  • Auto insurance prices: cooling premiums support the argument that drivers restore coverage.
  • Progressive policy growth: IAA's biggest customer. Slower growth eases the headwind on Copart.
  • DOJ disclosure: any real change in the wording of Copart's anti-money-laundering disclosure.

Ground rules

  • Copart's and IAA's terms of service likely restrict automated access. Read them and decide as a team. Keep volumes small (the defaults do), stay logged out, and use the data for your own research only.
  • The console never solves CAPTCHAs, rotates identities or disguises itself. When a site shows a bot check, it stops for the day and tells you. It checks robots.txt unless you turn that off in Settings; Copart's robots.txt allows the pages the scan uses.
  • Check your competition's rules on public information and AI use. Describe how you collected the data, and disclose that this tooling was built with AI assistance if the rules ask. The analysis and conclusions are yours.
  • Headlines and filing excerpts are for finding sources. Read and cite the originals.
