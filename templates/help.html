What the console does

The console is a small website that runs only on this Mac. It collects public data about Copart and its market, keeps every week of history in one database, compares each week with the one before and writes short notes about what changed. Nothing is sent anywhere except the requests it makes to collect data.

SourceWhat the console takesHow often
Copart's search pagesLots listed at every Copart location, and lots added in the last 7 daysEvery day
FRED and the BLSPrice indexes for repairs, parts, used cars and auto insurance, and miles drivenWeekly
SEC EDGARProgressive's monthly results, release excerpts, Copart's DOJ paragraph and full-text search hitsWeekly
Google News and trade pressHeadlines about Copart, IAA, salvage and alternative dataWeekly

With auto-update on, the Copart scan runs every day and the full update once a week, at the day and time set in Settings{% if schedule.installed %} ({{ schedule.when }}){% endif %}. The schedule runs even when the console's window is closed, as long as the Mac is on; if the Mac is asleep, the task runs when it wakes. You get a notification after each full update, and whenever Copart blocks a scan.

Your routine

WhenWhat to do
Any dayNothing. If a notification says Copart blocked the scan, fill in that day with Scan with me on Copart inventory, or wait for the next scan.
After the weekly notificationOpen This week and read the notes from the top. Act on anything marked Needs attention or To do, then check that everything under Data freshness says OK.
Before a deadlineOn Reports, download the Excel workbook and the charts. Check each number you plan to use against its source; see tracing a number.

To open the console, double-click Open CPRT Console.command and leave the Terminal window it opens. Closing that window stops the website but not the schedule.

Reading the screen

The task banner

Whenever a task runs, a banner at the top of every page shows its name, what it's doing and a moving bar. Details opens the task's log: every step it took, such as "Step 4 of 6: Copart scan". When a task finishes, the page reloads with the new data. If it fails, the banner says it didn't finish, and the last lines under Details say why. Stop recording ends a recorded session and saves it; Dismiss hides the banner.

Notes

The notes on This week are written by the console each time the page opens. They come in four kinds, most important first:

  • Needs attentionA real change or a problem, such as a significant move or new DOJ wording.
  • To doSomething only you can do, such as running a scan that's overdue.
  • Supports the longData consistent with the thesis, such as cooling insurance prices.
  • NoteContext worth knowing, such as the latest Copart totals.

Status badges

OK it worked. Failed it didn't; hover the badge to see why. Skipped it was left out, usually because a setting is missing. Not run yet it hasn't happened yet.

Tables, filters and buttons

  • Click a column heading to sort a table, and click it again to reverse the order. Type in a filter box to narrow the rows.
  • The dark button at the top right of a page is its main action. Buttons that start a task show the banner while they work.
  • "Download" links save a file, such as a PNG chart for your slides.
  • Colors always mean the same thing: blue is Copart, red is IAA, amber needs attention and green supports the long.

Insurer tracker

Built to measure what share of each insurer's salvage is listed on Copart rather than IAA. It's paused: Copart shows the seller on about 1 lot in 20 to logged-out visitors, and IAA's pages carry no lot data the console can read, so the split can't be measured from public pages. The page says so at the top and keeps its tables for reference.

News

Articles from Google News and a trade feed, for the searches in Settings. The score counts the alternative-data terms in each headline and summary ("market share", "volume", "tracker" and so on), so the highest scores are the articles most likely to cite data. The views show what's new this week, unread or starred. Star keeps an article and Mark read clears it. The console keeps headlines and links only, so read and cite the article itself.

What the data can't tell you

  • Copart's counts cover every kind of seller, not only insurers, because Copart hides sellers from logged-out visitors.
  • "Added in the last 7 days" leaves out lots that sold within the week. It's a consistent weekly measure, not a count of every arrival.
  • Copart's locations include offsite lots, so location counts run higher than yard counts.
  • There's no IAA data. Comparisons between Copart and IAA have to come from RB Global's filings or broker figures you cite, such as the JPM tracker.
  • The insurer split is paused until a site shows sellers.
  • Numbers read from filings are best effort, so check them against the filing.

When something goes wrong

You seeWhat it meansWhat to do
The banner says a task didn't finishThe task stopped with an error. Open Details; the last lines say why. If it isn't clear, copy them and ask for help.
"Copart showed a bot check"Copart's protection blocked the automated browser. Nothing to fix. The scan runs again at the next scheduled time, and Scan with me fills in that day.
A Failed badge under Data freshnessThat source failed last time. Hover the badge for the reason, then press Update on that row.
SEC filings say SkippedYour name or email is missing. Add them in Settings.
The browser says it can't connectThe console's Terminal window was closed. Double-click Open CPRT Console.command.
Auto-update never runsThe Mac was off, or the folder moved. Keep the folder out of Downloads, Desktop and Documents, then turn auto-update off and on again in Settings.
No notifications appearmacOS is blocking them. In System Settings, open Notifications and allow Script Editor.
A chart link says "Not Found"There isn't enough data for that chart yet. Wait for more scans or updates.
A page says "Internal Server Error"A bug in the console. Restart the console. If it happens again, copy the last lines of its Terminal window and ask for help.

Behind the scenes

  • The Terminal window runs the website at 127.0.0.1:5055, an address only this Mac can open.
  • Each task runs as its own background program, one at a time, and saves a log in the data folder under jobs/.
  • Auto-update is a macOS scheduled task (launchd). It runs the same tasks as the buttons, without the website.
  • Copart pages are loaded by a normal, logged-out Chromium browser with images turned off, with {{ S.pause_between_pages[0] }} to {{ S.pause_between_pages[1] }} seconds between pages.

Everything lives in the data folder, {{ config_data_dir }}. Copy that folder to back it up.

Glossary

Assignment
An insurer sending a totaled car to an auction platform.
Lot, or stock number
The platform's ID for one vehicle. Copart says lot; IAA says stock number.
Listed inventory
Lots currently for sale.
Total-loss frequency (TLF)
The share of claims where the car is written off rather than repaired.
Policies in force (PIF)
An insurer's active policies.
Combined ratio (CR)
An insurer's claims and expenses as a share of premiums. Under 100 means an underwriting profit.
Split
Copart's share of one insurer's listed lots: Copart lots divided by Copart plus IAA lots.
Paired states
States sampled on both sites in the same week. Only these count in the split.
Confidence interval
The range a share could plausibly fall in, given the sample size. The console shows 95% intervals.
Take rate, or revenue per unit
A platform's fees as a share of vehicle prices, or per vehicle sold.
ACV
Two meanings: actual cash value (a car's value before the accident), or ACV Auctions, which Copart agreed to buy.
IAA and RB Global
Insurance Auto Auctions, Copart's main rival, owned by RB Global (ticker RBA).
autoAstat and Yipit
Paid data providers the sell-side cites for Copart and IAA volumes.
robots.txt
A file where a website asks automated tools to stay out of some pages.
Bot check
A page a site shows instead of its content when it suspects an automated visitor. Copart and IAA use Imperva for this.
